Leak class against observer layer

Two questions decide what a compiler-level proof is worth, and they are usually run together into one. What leaks — the kind of secret-dependent thing the program did. Who is watching — how close the observer sits to the secret state. Neither answers the other, and a threat model needs both.

Along the first axis the list is short and closes. Along the second it is ordered: each ring inward means more direct access. Crossing them gives the grid below, and the grid is mostly empty — a class of leak only becomes visible from some ring inward, so the attack surface is a staircase rather than a rectangle.

What the MLIR pipeline actually closes

One cell, not two classes: timing × O2

O2 is a restatement of our leakage model almost word for word — branches, address classes, latency classes, nothing about operands — which is why the pipeline fits it exactly. The claim comes with three qualifiers, and none of them is small:

  1. Only the operations we can translate. Between 17% and 62% of operation mentions per compiler; the rest cannot be pushed through a proof at all.
  2. Only the steps with a checked specification. Three compilers have any; the other three have none.
  3. For the specification of a step, not for the compiler. No compiler was built here; every before/after pair is transcribed from the pass source with a citation.

And functional equivalence is not ours today. FCVD can prove it — upstream verify-pdl does, for PDL rewrites, and we report its verdict beside our own — but our own checker deliberately replaces the refinement criterion with false so that what it proves is about leakage. The functional column is therefore borrowed, not earned.

Two things the onion cannot say

Where the picture stops working

Active attacks have no ring. Glitching, lasers and Rowhammer do not observe — they change the computation and read the wreckage. A program can be perfectly constant-time and hand over the key after one induced fault. That needs a second axis, passive against active, not a ninth circle.

The circles are not nested in strength. They are ordered by directness of access, which is not the same thing. A remote observer at O1 with a million queries beats a physical observer at O6 with one trace, because averaging wins. And a probe at O6 cannot see the cache state of a neighbouring process, though it is drawn inside O3. Useful as a vocabulary; wrong as a lattice.

O5 is a boundary, not a level. A TEE or an HSM does not sit between O4 and O6 in strength — it declares part of the state unreachable and moves the question to whether the declaration holds.
